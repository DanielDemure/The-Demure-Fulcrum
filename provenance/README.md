# Provenance and Version History

## Origin of the concept

Daniel Demure developed the initial formulation of **The Demure Fulcrum** in private working notes dated December 2025. Those notes introduced the central intuition that a person under threat may attempt to preserve agency by changing the terms of the situation rather than selecting only among confrontation, escape, or immobilisation.

The first public repository version was committed on 2 January 2026 under the title *The Demure Fulcrum: Negotiation as a Fourth Meta-Option in Human Threat Response*. Git history preserves that complete version, including its original paper, research notes, references, and PDF.

## What provenance does and does not establish

Author-origin material establishes **priority and conceptual provenance**. It shows when and how the framework was formulated. It does not independently establish that the framework is empirically valid, biologically distinct, universal, or superior to existing models.

Accordingly, the revised paper cites external scholarship for scientific and historical claims. The author's original formulation is treated as the object being clarified and tested, not as evidence that its own claims are true.

## August 2026 reconstruction

The August 2026 revision changes the epistemic status and structure of the project:

- negotiation is framed as an **agency-preserving higher-order meta-response**, not as a demonstrated fourth autonomic reflex;
- strict interpersonal or institutional negotiation is separated from intrapersonal and existential extensions;
- observable boundary conditions, rival explanations, testable predictions, and disconfirmation criteria are added;
- cultural examples are reclassified as interpretive illustrations;
- Hexure is separated as a speculative application note;
- popular and placeholder references are replaced by a smaller scholarly bibliography;
- the paper is explicitly labelled a working paper that has not been peer reviewed.

## Archival access

No historical claim has been erased. Readers can inspect the repository's earlier commits to compare the original thesis with the reconstructed framework. This allows the project to evolve without presenting revision as though it had always been the original formulation.
