# Changelog

All notable public changes to The Demure Fulcrum are documented here.

## Unreleased — August 2026 scholarly reconstruction

### Changed

- Reframed negotiation from a claimed primary instinct to an **agency-preserving higher-order meta-response under threat**.
- Rewrote the working paper around explicit levels of analysis, an operational definition, boundary conditions, a formal `G → G′` model, six testable predictions, rival hypotheses, disconfirmation criteria, and a staged empirical research programme.
- Separated strict interpersonal or institutional negotiation from intrapersonal and existential extensions.
- Replaced simplified triune-brain language with a networked and context-sensitive account of threat, appraisal, regulation, and social cognition.
- Positioned Polyvagal Theory as contested and non-essential to the framework.
- Reframed cultural material as interpretive illustration rather than scientific evidence.
- Replaced the original reference list with a curated scholarly BibTeX database.
- Changed the repository licence for original scholarly content from MIT to CC BY 4.0.

### Added

- Threat-response and regulation research companion.
- Construct, measurement, formal-model, and experimental-design companion.
- Cultural and philosophical illustration companion.
- Separate Hexure speculative application note.
- Provenance statement, citation metadata, contribution guidance, and peer-review questions.
- Reproducible ReportLab PDF build, repository validator, and GitHub Actions workflow.

### Removed from the evidentiary core

- Claims that a twenty-trade anecdote validates the psychological theory.
- The Lord of War metaphor as a scientific or ethical foundation.
- OMAD, clothing routines, radical transparency, and personal doctrine as evidence.
- Popular, social-media, listicle, and placeholder sources used to support scientific claims.

## 0.1.0 — 2 January 2026

- Initial public repository.
- Original academic paper, PDF, and three research documents uploaded.
- Negotiation proposed as a fourth meta-option in human threat response.

The initial state remains available in Git history for transparent comparison.
